# Fate Round — Brand Voice & Style Guide (v2)

*Reverse-engineered from the copy you already ship — now covering both surfaces: marketing (`llms.txt`, homepage SEO, game landing pages) **and** in-product microcopy (buttons, empty states, errors, share text). v2 adds four things v1 missed: a product/UI voice section (§7), a real edge-and-safety boundary (§8), a spelling convention (§9), and a copy-paste microcopy library (§10). Approve or tweak, then we build against it.*

---

## 1. In one line

**Fate Round sounds like the funniest friend in the group chat who also happens to be organized.** Playful and a little savage, but always clear about how the thing actually works. Never corporate, never try-hard.

---

## 2. Who we're talking to

Friend groups, Discord and video calls, birthday parties, icebreakers, team socials, family game night, late-night group chats. People who want to start a game in ten seconds, not read a manual. Assume they're on a phone, half-distracted, and deciding in five words whether this is worth it.

---

## 3. The five voice pillars

**Playful, with an edge.** The reveals are the point. Copy leans into chaos: "Results get messy," "savage reveals, zero mercy," "until nobody's safe." We tease, we never bully. The edge is aimed at the *game*, never the reader.

**Ruthlessly clear.** Every playful line is followed by exactly how it works. "Anonymous votes, savage reveals, zero mercy" *and then* "vote for the friend who fits each prompt." Fun sells it; clarity closes it.

**Fast and punchy.** Short sentences. Fragments are fine. Em dashes to snap two ideas together — like this. If a word can be cut and the meaning survives, cut it.

**Frictionless-first.** The three magic promises appear constantly and should keep appearing: **no sign-up, no download, no app store**. Plus "create a game in seconds," "share a short code or link," "join from any browser." This is the core value prop — repeat it, don't bury it.

**Confidently plain.** "The classic party game, upgraded." No buzzwords, no "revolutionary," no "seamless synergy." Real nouns, real verbs. We say *smash, marry, kill* — not "make selections."

---

## 4. Rhythm & structure (the signature move)

The house formula for a hook is: **playful promise → mechanic → payoff.**

> "The classic party game, upgraded. Three faces land each round — your group assigns smash, marry, and kill. Results get messy."

> "'Most likely to…' prompts meet your actual friend group. Anonymous votes, savage reveals, zero mercy."

> "One person in the hot seat. Everyone else drops a compliment, observation, or roast. Take turns until nobody's safe."

Three beats. Tension then release. Use this shape for hero subtitles and section intros.

## 5. Words we use / words we don't

**Use:** smash, marry, kill (call the games what they're called), crew, group, vote, reveal, round, drop a message, no names just vibes, in seconds, free forever, roast, spicy, chaos, share a code.

**Avoid:** leverage, seamless, revolutionary, cutting-edge, solution, platform (in the product-y sense — "party game platform" is fine as a factual descriptor, not as a brag), users (say *players*, *friends*, *your group*), engage/engagement, elevate, unlock, empower.

**Naming:** Always "**Fate Round**" (two words, both capitalized). Call each game by its real/common name and its alias where useful — "Smash Marry Kill (also called Kiss Marry Kill)," "Whot — the Nigerian card classic."

## 6. Formatting conventions

- **Em dash** — is the house punctuation for snapping a mechanic onto a promise. Used liberally.
- **Sentence case** for most UI and headings, not Title Case screaming.
- **Emoji** used sparingly as functional labels (⚡ 📡 📱 🔗), one per feature — never sprinkled into prose.
- **Curly quotes** ('' "") in body copy.
- Keep paragraphs to 2–4 sentences. On landing pages, one idea per block.
- Numbers as digits when they're a selling point ("20+ game modes," "75-ball bingo," "first to 100 wins").

---

## 7. Tone by context

Voice is fixed; tone flexes. Same personality, dialled up or down by where the words appear.

**Hero / headlines:** Maximum playful. Land the joke, promise the chaos.
**How-it-works / steps:** Drop the edge, keep it warm and dead simple. "Pick a game, share the code, play."
**FAQs / trust ("is it free?", "do I need an account?"):** Straight, reassuring, zero snark. Answer in the first sentence.
**In-product microcopy (buttons, states, errors):** Warm and functional first, playful second (see §7b). Clarity always wins over a joke.
**Safety, moderation, reporting, anything sensitive:** Plain and sincere. No jokes, no edge. See §8.

### 7b. Product & UI voice (the surface v1 ignored)

Most of your words aren't on landing pages — they're buttons, empty lobbies, waiting screens, and error toasts. Your product copy is already good and on-voice; these are the rules that keep it that way.

**The pattern, pulled from your own UI:**
- **Empty states invite, they don't scold.** "No messages yet. Say hi!" · "No players joined yet" · "If this stays empty, ask the host to start the game." → State the fact, then give the next action, warmly.
- **Waiting states reassure with a wink.** "Answers locked in — waiting for everyone…" · "Everyone who claimed a name has submitted — ready to start" → Tell them what's happening and that it's fine.
- **Errors are calm and fixable — never blame the user.** "Check the code and try again." Say what to do next, not what they did wrong. No "Invalid input," no stack-trace tone, no "Oops!" theatrics.
- **Buttons are verb-first and short.** "Copy invite link," "Create a new game," "Leave." Two words where two words will do.

**Rules:**
1. Every empty/error state ends with the *next action*. Never a dead end.
2. Never blame or shame the player. The tone is "here's the fix," not "you did it wrong."
3. Save the edge for game content (prompts, reveals). Utility copy stays warm-plain — a locked-vote screen is not the place for a roast.
4. One idea per string. Microcopy is read at a glance on a phone mid-game.
5. Ellipses (…) for in-progress states, sentence case, no exclamation-mark spam (one is plenty).

## 8. The edge & safety boundary (where the fun stops being funny)

The voice is "playful with an edge aimed at the game, never the reader." That works — but Fate Round runs genuinely spicy modes (Smash Marry Kill, Smash or Pass, Date My Kid, Never Have I Ever) and **anonymous** surfaces (Anonymous Room, confessions, secret messages) where the same edge can curdle into harassment. This section draws the line so the brand stays fun without becoming a liability.

**The edge is aimed at the *game*, never a person.** "Results get messy," "zero mercy" — the target is the format's chaos, not any individual. Copy never tells players to be cruel to each other.

**Spicy is a flavour, not a free-for-all.** Modes can be bold, flirty, and a little savage. They are never sexually explicit, never degrading, and never punch at protected characteristics (race, religion, gender, sexuality, disability). "Savage reveals" ≠ "say something hateful."

**Anonymous ≠ consequence-free — and the copy should say so.** On anonymous surfaces, keep utility copy warm but add a light, non-preachy nudge toward decency where it fits ("no names, just vibes — keep it fun"). Moderation, reporting, and blocking copy is the one place the voice goes fully plain and sincere: clear, calm, zero jokes.

**Minors and anything near them: no edge, ever.** No sexualised framing, no adult content, in any copy that a younger audience could plausibly hit. "Date My Kid" and similar are played straight and light — parental-approval comedy, never anything creepy.

**The hard-line word list — never in Fate Round's voice:** slurs; sexual explicitness; content sexualising minors; encouragement of self-harm, bullying, or harassment; punching-down "jokes" at protected groups. No amount of "it's just a party game" overrides this.

**Quick test:** if a line would make the *target* of the joke feel genuinely unsafe rather than playfully called-out, it's over the line — rewrite it.

## 9. Spelling & localization — DECISION: American English

**Locked: American English for all UI and marketing prose.** It matches your largest search market (US), your `.com`, and most competitor copy. That means: *center, color, favorite, prioritize, canceled, gray, localization.* Keep game *names* and cultural terms authentic regardless ("Whot," "Naija house rules," "General Market") — localization applies to ordinary prose, not proper nouns or cultural vocabulary. Scrabble/Collins dictionary word-data is exempt (it's a real dictionary — leave it as-is).

**Known inconsistencies in the current product prose to sweep** (all British → American): "centre star" → "center star" (Scrabble landing + rules), "colour-group" → "color-group" (Monopoly rules — *note: verify this isn't intentional UK-Monopoly styling before changing*), "Prioritise" → "prioritize" (game-landing, participant-mode). Everything I write from here follows the American convention by default.

Other conventions: curly quotes (''  "") in prose; Oxford comma on; numerals for selling-point numbers ("20+," "first to 100"); em dash — no spaces around it — as the house turn.

## 10. Microcopy & CTA library (copy-paste)

So nobody reinvents the wheel. All on-voice, all reusable.

**Primary CTAs:** Create a free room · Start a game · Share the code · Send the link · Play now · Jump in
**Join / share:** Copy invite link · Send this to your group · One code, everyone's in · Share and play
**Empty states:** No players joined yet — share the code to fill the room · No messages yet. Say hi! · Nothing here yet — start a round to kick things off
**Waiting / loading:** Waiting for everyone… · Locking in votes… · Almost there — hang tight · Setting up the room…
**Errors (calm + fixable):** Check the code and try again · That room's not around anymore — start a fresh one · Lost the connection — reconnecting… · Couldn't join — double-check the code
**Success / reveal:** Locked in · You're in · And the votes are in… · Room's live — send the code
**Reassurance (the three promises, varied):** Free forever · No sign-up, ever · No download, no app · Plays right in your browser · Everyone joins from their own phone
